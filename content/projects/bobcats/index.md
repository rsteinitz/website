## At a glance

- **What:** A stable isotope study of bobcat prey preference in Southern California.
- **Why:** Drought can alter prey communities and predator foraging ecology, with potential consequences for carnivore resilience in changing landscapes.
- **How:** Using stable isotope data to examine bobcat trophic ecology during the 2010s California drought.

---

## Project overview

This project investigates prey preference and trophic ecology in bobcats (*Lynx rufus*) in Southern California during the prolonged California drought of the 2010s.

Using stable isotope analysis, the study examines how drought-associated environmental change may have influenced bobcat diet, prey use, and predator-prey dynamics. The project also contributes to broader efforts to interpret regional isotope baselines and understand how carnivores respond to ecological stressors in human-modified and drought-prone landscapes.

This work is conducted in collaboration with USGS and contributes to ongoing discussions within the Southern California Bobcat Working Group, which includes members from CDFW.
