## At a glance

- **What:** Exploring how early-life experiences relate to health and aging in mountain gorillas.
- **Why:** Understanding vulnerability and resilience across the lifespan can strengthen the scientific foundation for conservation.
- **How:** Combining non-invasive measures of physiology with observations of health and long-term life-history information.

---

## Connecting life experiences, physiology, and health

{{< figure src="gorilla-health-framework.png" caption="Conceptual overview: combining non-invasive biomarkers with observations and life-history context to study mountain gorilla health and aging." >}}

I use non-invasive physiology to explore how early-life adversity relates to health and aging in mountain gorillas. Physiological measurements offer a window into processes that are not always visible from behavior or appearance alone. Combining these measures with observations and life-history information helps me investigate how health changes over time and why individuals differ in their responses to adversity.

This work connects questions about aging and resilience with the needs of wildlife conservation. A better understanding of physiological variation can help researchers interpret changes in health and recognize vulnerability, contributing to the evidence that informs long-term monitoring and conservation efforts.

## My role

- **Research and analysis:** Develop physiological approaches and analyze data to investigate relationships among early-life experiences, health, and aging.
- **Connecting evidence:** Bring physiological measurements together with observations and life-history information to interpret patterns within and among individuals.
- **Collaboration and mentorship:** Work with research partners and mentor students and staff in physiological research, data analysis, and scientific interpretation.
